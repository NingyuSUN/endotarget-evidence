# Literature context for the eutopic stromal disease contrast

Purpose: place this project's results among the published transcriptome studies of the
eutopic endometrium in endometriosis and the methods literature they rest on, as material
for a manuscript's introduction and discussion. Date: 2026-11-02.

**How the sources were handled.** Candidate papers were identified from PubMed records
(searches and known citations; identifiers in
[`manifests/literature_candidates.tsv`](../manifests/literature_candidates.tsv)). For every
candidate, a free full text was sought through Europe PMC (open-access XML, PDF render) and
NCBI PMC author manuscripts; each attempt is recorded with URL, HTTP status, bytes, SHA-256
and time in [`manifests/literature_receipts.tsv`](../manifests/literature_receipts.tsv)
([`scripts/literature_fetch.py`](../scripts/literature_fetch.py)). Local copies live in the
git-ignored `reference/papers/PMID<id>/` folders and were converted to text once
([`scripts/literature_extract.py`](../scripts/literature_extract.py) for JATS XML; the
project's accuracy-first PDF extractor for the two PDF-only copies). Every number and claim
below marked **[full text]** was read from a local copy. Papers for which no free copy
exists are used at **[abstract]** level only, from their PubMed records, and are listed at
the end for manual download; their numbers are not relied on beyond the abstract.

## 1. Bulk transcriptome studies of eutopic endometrium, endometriosis versus controls

| Study | Design | Disease comparison and headline result | Relation to this project |
|---|---|---|---|
| Burney et al. 2007, *Endocrinology* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1210/en.2006-1692)) **[full text]** | Microarray (HG-U133 Plus 2.0), 37 specimens: 21 moderate/severe endometriosis (PE 6, ESE 6, MSE 9) versus 16 disease-free (PE 5, ESE 3, MSE 8); DEG rule 1.5-fold and BH-corrected p < 0.05 | Incomplete proliferative-to-early-secretory transition in disease, enhanced cellular survival and attenuated progesterone-induced down-regulation of DNA synthesis and mitosis genes; candidates FOXO1A, MIG6, CYP26A1 | The origin of the progesterone-resistance hypothesis tested at program level by the secretory program contrast; 5–9 donors per phase and arm |
| Aghajanova & Giudice 2010, *Reprod Sci* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1933719110386241)) **[full text]** | Microarray; 63 endometriosis donors (19 mild, 44 severe) in PE, ESE and MSE; 12 non-endometriosis donors used for immunohistochemistry only | Severe versus mild disease within each phase; VCAN and EGFR among the most up-regulated in severe disease | A within-disease severity contrast, not a case–control one; stages are mixed in the v1 cohorts |
| Fassbender et al. 2012, *Hum Reprod* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/humrep/des127)) **[full text]** | Microarray and proteomics, 49 biopsies: 31 with endometriosis (16 stage I–II, 15 stage III–IV; menstrual 14, early luteal 17) and 18 without (9 normal pelvis, 9 other pelvic pathology; menstrual 8, early luteal 10) | No differentially expressed mRNA between women with and without endometriosis regardless of stage or phase, while 925 and 1,087 genes differed between menstrual and early luteal endometrium within each group | An early bulk null with surgical controls, the same control definition as v1, and the same pattern as here: large cycle effects, no detectable disease effect |
| Tamaresis et al. 2014, *Endocrinology* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1210/en.2014-1490)) **[full text]** | Microarray, 148 archived samples: 34 normal pelvis (NE.NUPP), 35 other uterine/pelvic pathology (NE.UPP), 75 endometriosis (E); proliferative phase 20 / 15 / 29 | Classifier analysis; 21,946 probe sets differed between [E + NE.UPP] and NE.NUPP but only 214 between E and NE.UPP, with "remarkably few up-regulated probe sets in the proliferative phase in E vs NE.UPP"; few genes regulated in all phases | The closest bulk analogue of the v1 contrast (endometriosis versus other surgical pathology, proliferative phase) and the largest phase-resolved bulk comparison with a near-null result; the discovery atlas (Duempelmann 2026) cites this literature as reporting no significant disease differences |
| Houshdaran et al. 2016, *Biol Reprod* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1095/biolreprod.116.140434)) **[full text]** | Methylome and expression, severe endometriosis versus controls; PE 4 versus 6, ESE 5 versus 5, MSE 5 versus 5 | Most differentially methylated loci in the mid-secretory phase (137 CpG sites); proliferative-phase loci mostly gained methylation | Phase-dependence of disease differences, largest in the progesterone-dominant phase |
| Fung et al. 2018, *Sci Rep* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-29462-y)) **[full text]** | Microarray and eQTL mapping in endometrium from 229 women (two Melbourne clinics) | Strong cycle-stage effects (2,427 of 15,262 probes); the discovery atlas cites it among the large bulk studies that "reported no significant gene expression changes between women with and without endometriosis" | Large phase-stratified bulk null |
| Mortlock et al. 2020, *Hum Reprod* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/humrep/dez279)) **[full text]** | RNA-seq and eQTL in endometrium from 206 women (184 gynaecology, 22 IVF) | "A direct comparison between women with and without endometriosis showed no significant difference in endometrial gene expression"; after correction for cycle stage, no gene was significantly differentially expressed | The largest sequencing-based bulk null; genetic regulation, not disease status, explains expression variance |
| Gabriel et al. 2020, *Sci Data* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-020-00623-x)) **[full text]** | EndometDB: arrays from 115 patients and 53 controls, endometrium, peritoneum and 190 lesions, with age, stage, hormonal medication and phase | A queryable database rather than a single case–control test | A resource for a future bulk program-level replication; not used here |
| Poli-Neto et al. 2020, *Sci Rep* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-57207-y)) **[full text]** | Meta-analysis of public arrays (GSE4888, GSE6364, GSE7305, GSE51981) by phase and stage; DEGs at adjusted p < 0.05 and twofold; xCell deconvolution | Immune-cell scores differ by stage (stage I–II: activated DC, CD4 TEM, eosinophils, M1 macrophages, NKT higher); stroma scores did not differ from controls; CIBERSORT found no consistent immune differences | Reports composition-level, mostly immune, differences from bulk deconvolution; no stromal difference |
| Poli-Neto et al. 2021, *J Reprod Immunol* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jri.2021.103307)) **[full text]** | Meta-analysis restricted to proliferative-phase arrays from GSE4888, GSE6364, GSE7305, GSE7307, GSE51981 and GSE25628 (54 healthy, 59 endometriosis: 16 stage I–II, 43 stage III–IV; plus 12 chlamydial endometritis); batch correction by empirical Bayes; DEGs at 1.5-fold and FDR 5% | Thousands of DEGs versus healthy controls (stage I–II 2,817 up / 3,787 down; stage III–IV 1,008 / 2,318); shared immune cell types and DNA-repair down-regulation with endometritis | A proliferative-phase result opposite in size to the within-study nulls; it pools heterogeneous array datasets after batch correction and uses healthy (not surgical) controls, so cross-dataset structure and the control definition are candidate explanations that this project cannot test |
| Vallvé-Juanico et al. 2019, *Fertil Steril* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fertnstert.2019.08.060)) **[full text]** | Sorted macrophages from 6 endometriosis and 5 control secretory-phase biopsies; RNA-seq | Mφ1 proportion higher in endometriosis; DEGs within sorted populations | Cell-type-resolved immune differences; stromal fibroblasts not studied |

| Huang et al. 2023, *Clin Exp Immunol* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/cei/uxad029)) **[abstract]** | 138,057 cells, 6 minimal/mild endometriosis and 7 controls | Immune compartment: loss of the secretory-phase decrease in immune, NK and T cells in endometriosis | Small immune-focused case–control single-cell study |
| Marečková et al. 2024, *Nat Genet* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-024-01873-w)) **[full text]** | HECA: 313,527 cells (63 women) and a new 312,246-nucleus dataset (63 donors), natural cycles and exogenous hormones | States that "conflicting evidence exists about whether and to what extent the endometrium itself differs between those with and without endometriosis" and that "larger sample sets are now needed"; GWAS integration points to decidualized stromal cells and macrophages as the cell types expressing endometriosis-associated genes | Source of the v1 external cohort; the GWAS cell-type mapping asks which cells express risk genes, a different question from whether risk genes are disease-differential in stroma (the genetic set-level test) |

| Riaz et al. 2026, *Int J Mol Sci* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms27167440)) **[full text]** | Narrative review of single-cell proportion data | Only minor changes in stromal and epithelial proportions between normal and eutopic endometrium of patients, but a marked increase in immune cells | Consistent with no stromal-state shift and with immune changes being the reported composition signal |
| Wang et al. 2026, *Mol Hum Reprod* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/molehr/gaaf061)) **[full text]** | Methylation deconvolution of endometrial aspirates from 637 cases and 347 controls | Cell-type proportions differ between cases and controls (latent-factor analysis) | A large but methylation-based composition claim; cell types not resolved to stromal states |

## 3. Methods literature

- **Pseudobulk and pseudoreplication.** Squair et al. 2021, *Nat Commun* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-25960-2)) **[full text]**: methods that ignore variation between biological replicates are biased and prone to false discoveries; pseudobulk methods outperform cell-level methods. Thurman et al. 2021, *Bioinformatics* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btab337)) **[full text]**: mathematical justification of pseudobulk for multi-subject designs; cell-level methods have unacceptable FDR from pseudoreplication. Prieto León et al. 2025, *NAR Genom Bioinform* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/nargab/lqaf179)) **[full text]**: unwanted variation in pseudobulk analyses. The v1 design (one donor, one observation; limma-voom) follows this literature.
- **Compositional tests.** propeller, Phipson et al. 2022, *Bioinformatics* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btac582)) **[full text]**: biological replication and transformed proportions with empirical Bayes variance; scCODA, Büttner et al. 2021, *Nat Commun* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-27150-6)) **[full text]**: Bayesian compositional model for low replicate numbers. The composition test here is a propeller-style donor-level regression (logit and arcsine shares, CLR per state) on the v1 design.
- **Network propagation.** Cowen et al. 2017, *Nat Rev Genet* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrg.2017.38)) **[full text]**: propagation as a universal amplifier of genetic associations, with random walk with restart as one formulation and explicit discussion of topology (node-degree) bias and degree-aware corrections such as DADA. Picart-Armada et al. 2019, *PLoS Comput Biol* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1007276)) **[full text]**: 12 propagation algorithms on 22 diseases; "network propagation is still a viable approach ... but special care needs to be put on the validation strategy". Liu et al. 2020, *Bioinformatics* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btaa150)) **[full text]**: supervised learning on full network connectivity outperforms label propagation; predicting from generic metrics such as high degree "does not capture anything unique about specific functions or diseases". The v1 and positive-control finding that restart-random-walk propagation on STRING transfers worse than expression ranking, with hub genes dominating its lists, sits within these caveats.
- **Progesterone resistance and eutopic receptivity.** McKinnon et al. 2018, *Trends Endocrinol Metab* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tem.2018.05.006)) **[full text]**: large-scale eutopic expression studies indicative of progesterone resistance "are often contradictory", tabulated study by study, while suppressed progesterone receptor expression "is supported in ectopic but not eutopic tissue"; Patel et al. 2017, *Acta Obstet Gynecol Scand* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/aogs.13156)) **[full text]**: eutopic cells "fail to downregulate genes needed for decidualization", with the PR-A/PR-B balance and inflammation as mechanisms; Lessey & Kim 2017, *Fertil Steril* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fertnstert.2017.05.031)) **[full text]** and the counterpoint by Miravet-Valenciano et al. 2017, *Fertil Steril* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fertnstert.2017.06.002)) **[full text]**, which argues from oocyte-donation outcomes that receptivity is not impaired when healthy embryos reach the cavity; Zondervan et al. 2020, *N Engl J Med* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMra1810764)) **[full text]**: prevalence about 10% of reproductive-age women, progesterone resistance and chronic inflammation among the altered features, no clinically relevant biomarkers; Retis-Resendiz et al. 2025, *Cells* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/cells14050326)) **[full text]**: "the presence and underlying mechanisms of decidualization defects in the eutopic endometrium of women with endometriosis remain controversial". Prašnikar et al. 2020, *J Assist Reprod Genet* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10815-020-01833-3)) **[full text]**: synthesis of 21 omics studies; "knowledge of endometrium biology in endometriosis is fragmented due to heterogeneity of published data".

## 4. Where this project's results sit

1. **The donor-level null is the expected result of within-study designs, not an anomaly.** The phase-stratified bulk studies with surgical controls found few or no disease-differential genes (Fassbender 2012: none in 31 versus 18; Tamaresis 2014: 214 probe sets between endometriosis and other pelvic pathology against 21,946 between any pathology and a normal pelvis; Fung 2018 and Mortlock 2020: none after cycle-stage correction in 229 and 206 women), the HECA authors describe the eutopic evidence as conflicting, and the discovery atlas authors read their own 427 cell-type DEGs across 63 cell types as a minor impact consistent with those bulk nulls. The positive signatures come from small series in progesterone-dominant phases (Burney 2007: 5–9 per arm and phase), within-disease severity contrasts (Aghajanova 2010) and cross-dataset meta-analyses with healthy controls (Poli-Neto 2020, 2021: thousands of DEGs at 1.5-fold after batch correction). The split in the literature therefore runs along design lines (single cohort with surgical controls versus pooled datasets with healthy controls) at least as much as along platform lines, which is why a prespecified, within-cohort, cross-replicated design with stated detectable effects is informative even when it finds nothing. What this project adds is not the null but its calibration: a positive control showing the same estimator detects 7,367 and 3,986 phase genes on the same donors, per-gene minimum detectable effects (1.5-fold per test, 2.2–3.4-fold at the multiplicity faced in one cohort, 1.3–1.8-fold pooled), and interval-exclusion counts.
2. **Composition is the reported signal in the eutopic literature, and it is immune, not stromal.** Deconvolution and single-cell studies report altered immune cell abundance (Poli-Neto 2020; Bunis 2022; Huang 2023; Vallvé-Juanico 2019; Riaz 2026), while stroma scores and stromal/epithelial proportions change little. The composition test here asks the stromal-state question directly (decidualized-like share and author states on the v1 design) and finds no detectable shift, which is consistent with that literature; the immune compartment is outside the v1 lineage and is not addressed.
3. **Progesterone resistance remains unresolved at tissue level, and the program-level test is a different kind of evidence.** The reviews disagree about whether eutopic receptivity and decidualization are impaired; the secretory program contrast tests the hypothesis within the stromal lineage with a signature defined in the other cohort, and bounds any attenuation to below 14% of the secretory shift in discovery while leaving HECA uninformative beyond a 71% loss. It neither confirms nor refutes the whole-tissue findings; it says what a 22-donor stromal pseudobulk can and cannot see.
4. **Genetics.** HECA's GWAS integration asks which cell types express risk-associated genes (decidualized stroma and macrophages); the genetic set-level test here asks whether risk-associated genes are disease-differential in proliferative stromal pseudobulk, and finds they are not enriched. Both can be true: a cell type can express risk genes without those genes differing between cases and controls at this resolution.
5. **Methods.** The design follows the pseudobulk consensus; the compositional test follows propeller's logic; the propagation result is consistent with the benchmark literature's warnings about validation and degree. The novelty is procedural: prespecified freezes, receipts, a positive control and detectability bounds around a negative result.

## 5. Draft discussion paragraphs (manuscript material)

> Our finding that no gene differs between endometriosis and non-endometriosis donors in proliferative-phase stromal fibroblasts, in either of two public single-cell cohorts or when their precision is pooled, is consistent with the phase-stratified bulk studies of eutopic endometrium that used surgical controls within a single cohort, which reported few or no disease-differential genes once cycle stage was accounted for (Fassbender et al. 2012; Tamaresis et al. 2014; Fung et al. 2018; Mortlock et al. 2020), and with the discovery atlas authors' own reading of a "minor impact" of disease status (Duempelmann et al. 2026). The positive signatures came from small series in progesterone-dominant phases (Burney et al. 2007), from severity contrasts within disease (Aghajanova & Giudice 2010) or from meta-analyses that pool heterogeneous array datasets against healthy controls (Poli-Neto et al. 2020, 2021), and the HECA authors describe the eutopic evidence as conflicting (Marečková et al. 2024). What distinguishes the present analysis is that the null is calibrated: the same estimator detected 7,367 and 3,986 cycle-phase genes on the same donors and transferred a frozen 20-gene list across cohorts without a single sign change, so the absence of disease genes is a statement about effect sizes, which we bound at 1.5-fold per gene and 1.3- to 1.8-fold when both cohorts are pooled, rather than about the pipeline.

> The composition-level differences reported in eutopic endometrium are predominantly immune (Poli-Neto et al. 2020; Bunis et al. 2022; Huang et al. 2023), whereas stromal and epithelial proportions change little (Riaz et al. 2026). Within the stromal lineage, we found no detectable shift in the share of decidualized-like states or in any author-labelled state, with detectable odds ratios of about 1.9 and 2.1; the pseudobulk null is therefore not explained by a compensating change in state mixture of that size. Whether disease alters the secretory response of these cells, the progesterone-resistance hypothesis (Burney et al. 2007; McKinnon et al. 2018), we tested at program level with a signature defined in the other cohort and found no detectable attenuation, with a bound of 14% of the secretory shift in the discovery cohort and a wide interval in the external cohort. These bounds are what two 22-donor cohorts can say; they do not resolve the tissue-level controversy (Lessey & Kim 2017; Miravet-Valenciano et al. 2017; Retis-Resendiz et al. 2025).

> Finally, genetically implicated genes were not enriched for stromal disease statistics, pooled or per cohort. This does not contradict the HECA observation that decidualized stromal cells and macrophages express endometriosis-associated genes (Marečková et al. 2024): expression of risk genes by a cell type and case–control differences in that cell type are different questions, and the latter may act through cell types, phases, lesion tissue or regulatory scales that a proliferative-phase eutopic stromal pseudobulk does not sample. The network result, that restart-random-walk propagation on STRING transferred worse than expression ranking even when a strong program was present, is consistent with published cautions that propagation performance depends on validation design and is sensitive to node degree (Picart-Armada et al. 2019; Liu et al. 2020).

## 6. Coverage

All 36 candidates have a local full text. Twenty-seven were retrieved automatically
(Europe PMC open-access XML or PDF render, NCBI PMC author-manuscript XML); nine without a
free copy (Burney 2007, Fassbender 2012, Patel 2017, McKinnon 2018, Zondervan 2020,
Wang 2020, Poli-Neto 2021, Cowen 2017, Miravet-Valenciano 2017) were supplied by the
maintainer as PDFs, copied byte for byte into `reference/papers/PMID<id>/manual.pdf`,
checked against the supplied SHA-256 list and registered with route `manual_download` in
the receipts, with the downloader's own receipt files kept beside each copy
([`scripts/literature_register_manual.py`](../scripts/literature_register_manual.py)). All
nine PDFs had a text layer on every page; no OCR was needed and the extractor reported no
number disagreements. Every claim above is now marked **[full text]** except where a study
is cited only from a sentence of another local paper.

Publication records for all candidates were retrieved from PubMed; DOIs are linked above.
